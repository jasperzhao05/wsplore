The policy predicts where the ball must be caught, rather than chasing its current position.

First-layer units compute small signed paddle-to-intercept errors, direction and bounce indicators, and brick-lookahead preferences. Descending intercepts include the exact wall reflections and both legal catch columns. While ascending, the policy anticipates the earliest possible brick bounce and revises its forecast each step.

Second-layer units select the appropriate direction and flight phase, then turn the error into left, right, or no-op. On the final descending row, either safe catch position can be selected; a weighted lookahead through the three brick rows biases the outgoing angle toward remaining bricks. Otherwise, large logit margins make positioning nearly deterministic.
